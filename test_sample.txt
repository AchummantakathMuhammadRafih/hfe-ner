**Cross references.**
Cross references within the HFE report to other content within, such as other report sections, appendices, or separate documents, can be quite helpful. However, we suggest limiting cross references to content in separate documents because the inconvenience of navigating to these documents might decrease the likelihood that the reader will do so. One exception to this advice is including cross references to the comprehensive risk analysis and the complete user manual.
**Including user manual content.**
It is usually unproductive and unwelcomed by the intended audience to include a device's entire user manual as an HFE report attachment because it will likely be rather lengthy and contain information extraneous to the reviewers immediate, HFE-related interests. At most, include in an attachment content such as task prompts, troubleshooting guidance, and a summary of warnings. The exception to this rule might be when the user manual is very short (e.g., fewer than 10 pages total) and the advantage to providing a complete document outweighs the presence of content that might be unnecessary for the FDA's review. As suggested earlier, it might be helpful to cross-reference the user manual as a separate document, which is normally delivered to the FDA as part of a complete submission.
**Content repurposing.**
Feel free to liberally repurpose content from preceding HFE end products, such as the introductory material in the summative usability test plan in Section 8: Details of Human Factors Validation Testing. But, take care not to repeat content, such as describing the intended users in Section 2: Intended Device Users, Uses, Use Environments, and Training and then again in Section 8: Details of Human Factors Validation Testing, if the repurposed content also included the description of the intended users. â€¢

**Residual risk commentary.**
Manufacturers must consider the summative usability test results and develop their residual risk commentary. This commentary analyzes the residual risk posed by use problems (e.g., use errors) that occurred during the summative usability test. We consider Section 8: Details of Human Factors Validation Testing to be the appropriate location for this commentary. For example, consider presenting the residual risk commentary below the root cause analysis of a given use error. Such commentaries will always assert that the use problem does not indicate a compelling need for a UI design modification, that the use-related risk is acceptable, and that the UI design can remain "as is." After all, if the assessed level of risk was deemed unacceptable, the manufacturer would not proceed to apply to the FDA for a device clearance or approval. Rather, there would be a need for design modification and some degree of design revalidation.
Take care to avoid suggesting that there is no need for design modification because of considerations such as the expenses, time delays, and inconvenience associated with making such modifications, which are not a regulator's concerns per se.
**Report attachments.**
As stated in the FDA's HFE guidance document, include the full summative usability test plan and a sample of all scripts and forms (e.g., test administrator's guide) used during the summative usability test as an attachment to the HFE report. Also, be sure to include any attachments to the report that might provide useful context to content referenced earlier in the document. Examples include user profiles, use environment descriptions, training materials (e.g., a training competency checklist), and user documentation (as appropriate).
